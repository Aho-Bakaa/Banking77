# Project Context: Reasoning-Distilled Banking Disambiguator & Intent Router

## 1. Problem Statement

In conversational banking and fintech customer support, automated intent routing and resolution frequently break down due to **premature commitment to the wrong intent** (overconfident misclassification).

### The Challenge of Fine-Grained Banking Intents (Banking77)
The [PolyAI Banking77 dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/PolyAI/banking77) consists of 13,083 customer service queries spanning **77 fine-grained intents**. Many of these intents are tightly coupled "semantic siblings" sharing significant vocabulary and context:

| Sibling Cluster | Closely Related Intents | Typical Confusion Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Card Failures** | `card_not_working`, `declined_card_payment`, `card_declined_abroad`, `pin_blocked` | Unspecified terminal error vs. incorrect PIN vs. regional block |
| **Card Security** | `lost_or_stolen_card`, `compromised_card`, `card_swallowed` | Physical street loss vs. ATM retention vs. digital credential compromise |
| **Transfers** | `pending_transfer`, `failed_transfer`, `transfer_not_received_by_recipient` | Normal settlement delay vs. network failure vs. incorrect beneficiary details |
| **Identity & Access** | `verify_my_identity`, `verify_source_of_funds`, `unable_to_verify_identity` | Routine KYC onboarding vs. compliance AML triggers |

### Consequences of Misclassification in Banking
- **Severe User Experience Degradation:** A customer reporting an incorrect PIN prompt (`pin_blocked`) might have their card permanently canceled if the bot overconfidently predicts `compromised_card`.
- **Operational Cost Spikes:** Routing genuine self-service requests into human queues or misrouting between Tier-1 and Fraud departments adds substantial cost and response lag.

### The Trade-Offs in Existing Approaches
1. **Traditional Classifiers & System 1 Discriminative Models (e.g., RoBERTa, Jev):**
   - Fast and hallucination-free for distinct classes.
   - **Limitation:** Incapable of dynamic natural-language generation. When confidence is split across sibling intents, they cannot construct intelligent, context-aware clarification questions—they must fall back to rigid, mechanical menu prompts.
2. **Off-the-Shelf Frontier Models (e.g., GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini Pro):**
   - High reasoning capability, but prompt-heavy (requiring extensive intent definitions), expensive at enterprise scale ($10k+ / month at volume), and introducing high latency (>1.5s TTFT).

---

## 2. Project Objective

Build and fine-tune a **Small Language Model (SLM)** distilled with **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces** generated by frontier models to:
1. **Think Before Deciding:** Use `<thought>` tags to dissect customer pain points, compare sibling intents, and eliminate false candidates before concluding.
2. **Graceful Disambiguation:** Differentiate between clear queries (which are resolved directly to an intent) and inherently ambiguous queries (which trigger targeted, natural clarification questions).
3. **Cost-Effective & Edge-Deployable:** Achieve frontier-level reasoning accuracy at low inference cost and sub-100ms latency, trainable within free Kaggle/Colab GPU tiers (QLoRA).

---

## 3. End-to-End Pipeline & Execution Steps

### Phase 1: Synthetic Reasoning Dataset Generation (Distillation)
- **Base Data:** Load the standard `PolyAI/banking77` dataset splits (train: 10,008 samples, test: 3,080 samples).
- **Frontier Teacher Prompting:**
  - Send queries in batches to a frontier LLM(you).
  - For each sample, generate:
    1. `<thought>`: Step-by-step reasoning identifying clues, sibling intents, and elimination criteria.
    2. `decision`: Structured verdict (`resolved` or `ambiguous`).
    3. `output`: The intent label (if resolved) or a natural, banking-compliant follow-up question (if ambiguous).
- **Synthetic Ambiguity Augmentation:**
  - Specifically synthesize 1,000–2,000 truncated/ambiguous banking utterances (e.g., *"My card failed today"*, *"Why was I charged an extra fee?"*) paired with gold clarification questions.

### Phase 2: Data Formatting & Quality Filtering
- Format dataset into standard ChatML / instruction format:
  ```json
  {
    "messages": [
      {
        "role": "system",
        "content": "You are an expert banking intent router. Analyze the customer query, evaluate sibling intents in <thought> tags, and output a JSON decision."
      },
      {
        "role": "user",
        "content": "I inserted my card at the ATM outside, the screen turned black, and the card didn't come out."
      },
      {
        "role": "assistant",
        "content": "<thought>\nCustomer mentions an ATM transaction where the card was retained by the machine.\n- Evaluate 'card_not_working': Not a chip or swipe failure.\n- Evaluate 'lost_or_stolen_card': Card is physically retained inside an ATM, not misplaced.\n- Exact match: 'card_swallowed'.\n</thought>\n{\"status\": \"resolved\", \"intent\": \"card_swallowed\"}"
      }
    ]
  }
  ```
- Filter out malformed JSON outputs, hallucinated intents outside the 77 official labels, and low-quality reasoning traces.

### Phase 3: Fine-Tuning & Gate Training
- **Base Model Selection:** `Qwen/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct`
  - Fully open weights (Apache 2.0, zero gated repo barriers on Kaggle).
  - Best-in-class reasoning among ~3B SLMs.
  - Native ChatML structure, low VRAM footprint (~5.5 GB in 4-bit NF4).
- **Classifier Gate Training (`scripts/03_train_gate.py`):**
  - Trains a lightweight System-1 binary router (`fast` vs `think`) on `gate_labels.jsonl`.
  - Dense `bge-small` embeddings + TF-IDF with balanced logistic regression.
  - 5-fold CV ROC-AUC: **0.7880**; calibrated threshold `0.227` guarantees **~90% recall on hard/ambiguous `think` queries**.
  - Artifact: `data/processed/gate_classifier.joblib`.
- **QLoRA SLM Training Pipeline (`scripts/03_train_qlora.py` and `notebooks/kaggle_train_qlora.ipynb`):**
  - Effective batch size: 16 (per-device 4, gradient accumulation 4).
  - LoRA configuration: rank $r=16$, $\alpha=32$, dropout $0.05$ targeting all linear projection layers (`q, k, v, o, gate, up, down`).
  - Adaptive learning rate: Cosine decay with 5% warmup (`lr = 2e-4`).
  - Step-wise evaluation every 100 steps on `val.jsonl`.
  - Early stopping: Halts if `eval_loss` stalls for 3 consecutive evaluations (patience 3).
  - Checkpoint saving: Saves every 100 steps (keeps last 3), permitting resumption across Kaggle sessions.
  - Expected training time on Kaggle T4: ~35–45 minutes for 3 epochs.

### Phase 4: Evaluation & Benchmarking
Evaluate across three distinct dimensions:
1. **Sibling Intent Accuracy:** Measure Top-1 classification accuracy specifically on confusing clusters (e.g., Card Failure cluster vs. Transfer cluster).
2. **Ambiguity Handling:** Measure precision/recall of flagging genuinely ambiguous queries vs. overconfidently guessing.
3. **Comparative Evaluation:**
   - Base 3B Zero-Shot / Few-Shot (Baseline).
   - Fine-Tuned 3B SLM (Our model).
   - Frontier Model (Upper bound reference for quality vs. cost/latency).
